1. Introduction
1.1. Purpose
This Software Requirement Specification (SRS) defines the functional and non-functional requirements for Smart Scheduling. The document translates the approved project scope into implementable and testable requirements for a single F&B store. It is intended to serve as the common baseline for analysis, design, development, integration, testing, and acceptance activities.
The purposes of this document are:
• Provide the Scrum Master and project team with a clear scope and requirement baseline for estimation and Sprint planning.
• Describe system stakeholders, functional requirements, business rules, and detailed use cases so developers and designers can implement consistent behavior.
• Provide testers with pre-conditions, post-conditions, main flows, alternative flows, exception flows, and measurable quality targets for test design.
• Reserve the required analysis and design diagram sections from the template; diagrams will be inserted separately after review and approval.

2. PROJECT OVERVIEW
2.1. Project Description
Smart Scheduling is a Web + Mobile workforce scheduling system designed for one small or medium F&B store, such as a cafe, restaurant, or milk-tea shop with many part-time employees. The system digitizes the workflow from employee availability registration, shift requirement setup, manual or automatic schedule generation, Manager review and publication, leave/swap/replacement handling, GPS attendance, timesheet processing, and operational dashboard/reporting.
The Web application is primarily used by Admin and Manager, while Staff primarily use the React Native Mobile App. Laravel/Microsoft SQL Server form the core system of record. Redis supports queue/cache processing. Automatic scheduling is delegated to an independent Python/FastAPI service using Google OR-Tools CP-SAT. The optional AI Assistant is separated from the Scheduling Engine and is limited to explanation, summary, and advisory support based on data verified by the core backend.
The official implementation scope is a single F&B store. Multi-store, multi-tenant, cross-store scheduling, service-plan quotas, full payroll/tax processing, continuous GPS tracking, and autonomous AI personnel decisions are outside the MVP scope.
2.2. Business Problems
• Part-time staff availability changes frequently, making manual schedule preparation time-consuming and error-prone.
• Managers must simultaneously consider availability, approved leave, required positions/skills, staffing headcount, working-hour limits, rest time, and unexpected absences.
• Schedules maintained through spreadsheets and chat messages can create duplicated assignments, missed updates, unclear responsibility, and difficulty tracing changes.
• Unexpected leave or last-minute shift gaps require managers to manually search for replacement staff and can lead to double claims if the process is not concurrency-safe.
• Planned schedules alone do not reflect actual working time, so businesses need a reliable attendance-to-timesheet process for working-hour monitoring and labor-cost estimation.
• A failure in an optimization or AI component must not interrupt essential store operations such as employee management, manual scheduling, attendance, and reporting.
2.3. Business Need
• Centralize employee, skill, availability, shift, schedule, leave, attendance, and timesheet data in one authoritative system.
• Reduce manual scheduling effort by generating constraint-based draft schedules while keeping the Manager responsible for review and publication.
• Maintain schedule validity by enforcing hard constraints and reporting unfilled positions when available staff cannot safely cover demand.
• Provide controlled leave, shift-swap, Open Shift, and replacement workflows that revalidate eligibility before changing official assignments.
• Record actual attendance through event-time GPS checks and transform attendance into auditable timesheets.
• Provide operational dashboards, PDF/Excel reports, notifications, and optional AI explanations without making the core application depend on AI.

2.5.6. Software Quality Attributes
The quality attributes below define measurable or verifiable expectations for the MVP. Measurements are evaluated in the agreed academic test environment and are not claims of production-scale service-level agreements.
2.5.6.1. Usability
• The Web and Mobile interfaces shall use consistent terminology for Employee, Availability, Shift, Draft Schedule, Published Schedule, Leave, Attendance, and Timesheet.
• Primary Staff tasks - viewing schedule, registering availability, check-in/check-out, leave, and shift requests - should be reachable through direct navigation without requiring administrative knowledge.
• Validation errors shall identify the affected field or rule and provide actionable correction guidance.
• Draft schedules, published schedules, unfilled positions, pending requests, and approved records shall be visually distinguishable to reduce operational ambiguity.
2.5.6.2. Reliability
• Laravel and Microsoft SQL Server are the core system of record. Failure of the Scheduling Service or AI Service shall not make core employee, manual scheduling, attendance, timesheet, dashboard, and report functions unavailable.
• Auto-scheduling jobs shall expose explicit lifecycle states such as queued/running/succeeded/failed or equivalent, so a timeout is not mistaken for a successful schedule.
• Open Shift assignment shall be concurrency-safe: one vacancy can be assigned successfully to only one Staff member.
• Critical write operations such as publishing a schedule, accepting an Open Shift, and approving/correcting attendance shall use transactional integrity appropriate to the selected implementation.
2.5.6.3. Security
• Protected API functions shall require authentication and server-side RBAC authorization for Admin, Manager, or Staff as appropriate.
• Passwords shall be stored using one-way password hashing; plaintext passwords shall not be stored in the application database.
• JWT/session handling shall include expiry and validation; authorization decisions shall not rely only on client-side UI hiding.
• User inputs shall be validated and parameterized ORM/query techniques shall be used to reduce common injection risks.
• GPS coordinates shall be collected only for attendance events in the MVP; continuous employee location tracking is not permitted.
• Audit-relevant changes such as schedule publication, account status changes, attendance corrections, and important configuration changes shall be traceable.
2.5.6.4. Maintainability
• Core business logic shall be organized in Laravel modules/services with clear separation from the optional Scheduling and AI services.
• The Laravel-to-Scheduling and Laravel-to-AI interfaces shall use documented JSON/API contracts so services can be tested independently.
• Scheduling constraints shall be implemented as identifiable rules with test datasets that allow individual hard constraints to be verified.
• Source code shall follow the project coding standards for React/JavaScript, Laravel/PHP, and Python, with version control in Git/GitHub.
2.5.6.5. Portability
• The Manager/Admin Web application shall run on modern desktop browsers used in the project test environment and use responsive layouts for practical screen sizes.
• The Staff Mobile App shall be implemented with React Native using JavaScript/JSX and shall access business functions only through the Laravel REST API.
• Docker/Docker Compose shall be used for repeatable local/test deployment of core backend, database/cache, Scheduling Service, and optional AI Service where applicable.
2.5.6.6. Performance
• Core API endpoints target P95 response time below 500 ms for ordinary CRUD/read operations in the defined test environment, excluding long-running external/optimization jobs.
• The Scheduling Engine target is approximately 15 seconds or less for the agreed academic dataset of about 20-30 employees for a weekly scheduling period, subject to configured solver time limit.
• Automatically generated schedules must have zero hard-constraint violations in the validation test suite.
• The system shall return an explicit timeout/failure state when an optional service exceeds its configured timeout, instead of blocking core requests indefinitely.
• Report/export jobs that exceed normal request time should be executed asynchronously when the implementation requires it.

Appendix A: Glossary
Term
Definition
Admin
Platform administrator responsible for accounts, access, service health, and permitted system configuration.
Manager
Store manager who manages staff, shifts, schedules, attendance, timesheets, reports, and operational decisions.
Staff
Employee who uses the Mobile App for availability, schedule viewing, attendance, leave, shift requests, timesheets, and notifications.
Availability
Time intervals submitted by Staff indicating when they are available to work.
Shift
A defined work period with start/end times and staffing requirements.
Shift Requirement
Required headcount, position, skill, skill level, or priority associated with a shift.
Draft Schedule
A proposed schedule that is not official until reviewed and published by the Manager.
Published Schedule
The official schedule visible to Staff after Manager approval.
Hard Constraint
A scheduling rule that must not be violated, such as approved leave, overlap prevention, required skill, or work-hour limits.
Soft Constraint
A preference optimized when possible, such as fairness or reduced overtime, without overriding hard constraints.
Open Shift
A vacant assignment offered to eligible Staff for claiming or replacement.
Attendance
Actual check-in/check-out events captured for a Staff member.
Timesheet
Approved actual working-time record derived from attendance and applicable rules.
Geofence
Allowed geographic radius around the store used to validate GPS attendance events.
OR-Tools CP-SAT
Google OR-Tools constraint programming solver used by the Scheduling Service.
Scheduling Service
Optional Python/FastAPI service that calculates constraint-based draft schedules.
AI Assistant
Optional advisory service that explains verified operational data and does not make official HR decisions.
Graceful Degradation
Architecture behavior in which core Laravel/Microsoft SQL Server functions remain available when optional Scheduling or AI services are unavailable.
RBAC
Role-Based Access Control used to restrict functions according to Admin, Manager, and Staff roles.
JWT
JSON Web Token used by the API for authenticated access sessions.
