Problem Statement
Vietnam’s F&B market is large but increasingly demanding in terms of operational efficiency. According to the Vietnam F&B Market Report 2025 by iPOS.vn and Nestlé Professional, Vietnam had approximately 329,500 F&B service outlets in 2025, up only 2.0% from 2024. More than 50,000 F&B outlets closed during the first half of 2025 under cost pressure, showing a strong market screening process in which businesses with weaker operations are more likely to exit. The same report estimates total F&B industry revenue at more than VND 726.5 trillion in 2025, indicating that the market remains large while competition increasingly depends on operational effectiveness.
This context is especially relevant to independent restaurants, cafés, tea shops, and other small or medium F&B stores, which are the target users of Smart Scheduling. Independent F&B businesses accounted for 91.7% of sector revenue in 2025, while the market continued to move toward more professional and standardized operations. The report also shows that 22.23% of surveyed businesses considered rising labor costs without corresponding productivity gains to be a barrier to profit margins. Part-time labor creates an additional management challenge: the report specifically notes that standardizing employment obligations for part-time workers is becoming more important and can create financial and administrative pressure for F&B businesses.
At the store level, these pressures appear in daily workforce operations. Managers must collect changing staff availability, ensure each shift has enough employees and required skills, avoid overlapping assignments, respect working-hour and rest rules, respond to sudden leave, and verify actual attendance. When these activities are handled through spreadsheets, chat messages, or informal manual processes, the manager must repeatedly reconcile availability, skills, staffing needs, leave status, and actual working time. This makes schedule changes slow, increases the risk of inconsistent assignments, and makes labor-cost control and performance reporting more difficult.
Smart Scheduling addresses this operational problem with a Web and Mobile workforce-management ecosystem for F&B. Laravel and Microsoft SQL Server form the always-available core system for employee management, availability, manual scheduling, leave, attendance, timesheets, dashboards, and reports. A separate Python/OR-Tools Scheduling Service generates optimized draft schedules using Constraint Programming, while an AI Assistant acts only as an optional decision-support layer. Both intelligent services are fault-isolated and asynchronous: if AI or the solver is unavailable, the core workflow continues to operate, and the Manager can still create or edit schedules manually. The Manager always makes the final operational decision.

Objectives and Scope
Objectives
The project objectives are defined based on the SMART criteria:
Specific: Build Smart Scheduling as a Web and Mobile workforce-management system for small and medium F&B businesses. The system will support three operational roles: Admin, Manager, and Staff. Core functions include role-based account and employee management, employee skills and contracts, weekly availability, shift requirements, manual and automatic scheduling, schedule review/publishing, leave and shift-swap processing, GPS check-in/check-out, timesheets, dashboards, reports, notifications, and an optional AI Assistant. The automatic Scheduling Service will use Python + OR-Tools CP-SAT and return draft suggestions rather than directly publishing a schedule.
Measurable: Core APIs must remain functional when AI Service or Scheduling Service is stopped. For the target test dataset of approximately 20–30 employees and a weekly schedule, the solver will use a configurable time limit of 15 seconds. A valid generated schedule must contain zero hard-constraint violations. When staffing is insufficient, the engine must not fail silently; it must identify unfilled shifts through slack variables and return the best feasible draft together with shortage information. Unauthorized role access test cases must be rejected. Concurrent Open Shift claiming must result in only one successful assignment. Attendance and approved Timesheet calculations must match the defined test cases, and core API P95 response time should remain below 500 ms in the agreed test environment.
Achievable: The core platform will use React.js for the management Web application, React Native (JavaScript/JSX) for the Staff mobile application, Laravel for the REST API and business rules, Microsoft SQL Server for relational data, and Redis for queues and cache. The Scheduling Service will be implemented as an independent Python/FastAPI service using OR-Tools. Long-running scheduling and AI requests will run asynchronously through jobs with status records, timeout, retry, and circuit-breaker behavior. AI does not require training a custom model for the MVP; it will call predefined, permission-checked backend endpoints and explain verified data.
Relevant: The project targets operational efficiency in Vietnam’s F&B sector, where iPOS.vn reports 329,500 service outlets in 2025 and continuing pressure to professionalize operations. The report identifies labor cost/productivity as a profit-margin barrier for 22.23% of surveyed businesses [1]. Smart Scheduling therefore focuses on improving workforce allocation, staffing visibility, attendance verification, and management responsiveness rather than building a general-purpose HR or accounting platform.
Time-bound: Development, integration, testing, and deployment will be completed within the Capstone schedule. Implementation will prioritize the operational MVP: authentication and role-based access control, employee/skill management, availability, shift management, manual scheduling, OR-Tools scheduling, publishing, leave, GPS attendance, timesheets, and basic dashboard/reporting. Shift-swap, Open Shift, and the optional AI Assistant follow after the core workflow is stable; workforce-demand forecasting is Future Scope.
Scope
In Scope (The system will support): Smart Scheduling focuses on workforce scheduling and shift operations for small and medium F&B businesses. The Capstone scope supports one F&B store only. All scheduling, attendance, timesheet, and reporting data belong to this single store; multi-store and multi-tenant operation are deferred to future scope. The main scope is divided into the following functional groups:
Account and Access Control Management
Authentication and RBAC: Secure authentication and role-based authorization for Admin, Manager, and Staff. Each protected function is restricted according to the user role.
Account lifecycle: Admin manages system user accounts and access; Manager manages employee profiles for the single store. Disabled accounts retain historical scheduling, attendance, and timesheet data.
Employee, Contract, and Skill Management
Employee management: Authorized Manager users can create and update employee profiles, employment type, position, skills and skill levels, contract information, and effective pay rates.
Staff self-service: Staff can view permitted personal information, maintain basic profile data, and use the Mobile App for availability, schedule, attendance, leave, swap, and notification functions.
Availability, Shift, and Schedule Management
Availability registration: Staff register weekly available time ranges before a Manager-defined deadline. A shift is normally assignable only when it falls completely within the employee’s available period.
Automatic Scheduling Engine: Laravel creates a SchedulingRun and sends a job asynchronously through Redis. The Python/OR-Tools service optimizes assignments using employee availability, approved leave, skills/skill levels, contract limits, rest time, consecutive shifts, store operating hours, and shift staffing requirements.
Schedule review, manual editing, and publishing: Solver output is stored as a Draft/Suggestion. The Manager reviews and may edit the schedule before publishing. Hard-constraint violations are always blocked; only allowed soft-constraint warnings may be overridden and audited. Published schedules are versioned.
Schedule notifications: Staff receive notifications after publication and when their published assignment changes.
Leave, Shift Swap, Open Shift, and Staff Shortage Support
Shift Swap: Staff can request a direct swap. Before Manager approval, the system revalidates availability, skill, leave, overlap, working-hour, and rest constraints.
Open Shift Claim: When a shift is unfilled or vacant, eligible Staff may claim it first-come, first-served. Laravel revalidates eligibility before commit; transactions and Optimistic Locking prevent double booking.
Fallback candidate filtering: If the Python Scheduling Service is unavailable, Laravel can still filter replacement candidates using core rules such as active status, availability, required skill, approved leave, overlap, working-hour limits, and rest time; Manager confirmation remains required.
Attendance, Timesheet, and Decision Support
GPS Attendance: Staff check in/out through the Mobile App. The Backend validates store geofence, GPS accuracy, server time, and configured attendance windows. GPS is collected only for attendance events, not for continuous tracking.
Timesheet: Actual working time is calculated from attendance data and configured rules such as grace period, break, rounding, and approved overtime. Approved Timesheet data—not merely planned schedule hours—is used for labor summaries.
AI Assistant: AI is an optional support layer that calls predefined, permission-filtered Laravel endpoints to explain verified statistics, scheduling shortages, and candidate information. AI has no direct write access to the production database and cannot make final reward, penalty, salary, promotion, or official scheduling decisions. If GPT or another external LLM is used, only the minimum authorized data is sent, and every generated answer is treated as an advisory suggestion requiring human review.
Dashboard, Reporting, Monitoring, and Notifications
Dashboard and reports: Laravel calculates operational indicators directly from Microsoft SQL Server, including staffing status, attendance, working hours, estimated labor cost, pending requests, and scheduling-run status. Reports can be exported to PDF and Excel.
Service monitoring: Admin can view health status for the Core API, database, Redis, Scheduling Service, and AI Service.
Notifications: Queue-based notifications support schedule publication/change, shift reminder, leave result, swap request, replacement offer, and availability deadline.
Out of Scope / Deferred Scope: To keep the Capstone implementation feasible, the following functions are not part of the required MVP or are limited to decision-support level:
Fully autonomous AI decisions: AI cannot publish schedules, directly modify official employee records, or automatically make employment decisions. Human confirmation remains mandatory.
Full enterprise HRIS: Recruitment, insurance processing, benefits administration, formal performance appraisal, and complete employee lifecycle management are outside the core scope.
Complete payroll and accounting: The system provides approved working-hour and estimated labor-cost data, but it does not calculate official tax, insurance, deductions, or statutory payroll.
Continuous employee location tracking: GPS is used only at attendance events; the system does not track Staff locations continuously.
Multi-store and cross-store scheduling: Support for multiple stores/branches, cross-store assignment, and centralized multi-store management is deferred to Future Scope.
Business-demand forecasting in the MVP: Revenue, order volume, customer traffic, inventory, and holiday-demand prediction are deferred until sufficient historical operational data is available.
Automatic schedule publication without Manager confirmation: Scheduling results remain drafts until an authorized user reviews and publishes them.
Guaranteeing perfect staffing when resources do not exist: If eligible Staff are insufficient, the system reports unfilled positions/shifts instead of violating hard constraints or claiming that every shift can always be fully covered.
Key Features & Requirements
Key Features (Main Functions)
Authentication and RBAC for Admin, Manager, and Staff.
Employee, contract, effective pay-rate, position, skill, and skill-level management with history preservation.
Weekly availability registration with configurable deadlines and schedule periods.
Constraint-based Scheduling Engine using Python + OR-Tools CP-SAT with asynchronous jobs, time limits, unfilled-shift slack, and conflict information.
Manual schedule editing, versioned publishing, and staff notifications; core scheduling remains usable even if the optimization service is offline.
Direct Shift Swap with full eligibility revalidation before Manager approval.
Open Shift Claim / replacement handling with transaction or Optimistic Locking to prevent simultaneous double assignment.
Leave Management and emergency replacement support with a Laravel rule-based fallback when the Scheduling Service is unavailable.
GPS check-in/check-out, Attendance verification, and approved Timesheet calculation based on actual work data.
Dashboard and PDF/Excel reporting calculated by Laravel/ Microsoft SQL Server without depending on AI.
Optional AI Assistant for verified data explanation and decision support through permission-controlled backend functions.
Responsive React Web for Admin/Manager plus Mobile App for Staff, with queue-based notifications and service health monitoring.
Requirements
Functional Requirements (FR):
FR01 – Authentication & Account Lifecycle: Secure login, refresh/session handling, account disabling, and credential recovery through configured channels.
FR02 – Authorization & Role-Based Access Control: Enforce RBAC for Admin, Manager, and Staff; unauthorized role access must be rejected.
FR03 – Employee / Contract / Skill Management: Maintain employee profiles, employment type, positions, skill levels, contracts, effective pay rates, and active/inactive status.
FR04 – Availability Management: Staff register available time ranges; Manager configures registration deadlines and scheduling periods.
FR05 – Shift & Schedule Management: Manager defines shifts, required headcount, positions/skills, operating hours, and scheduling rules; manual scheduling is always available.
FR06 – Automated Scheduling: Create asynchronous Scheduling Run jobs and generate draft assignments with OR-Tools while enforcing hard constraints and minimizing weighted soft-constraint penalties.
FR07 – Schedule Review & Publish: Store solver results as draft suggestions; validate manual edits; version and publish an official schedule only after authorized confirmation.
FR08 – Leave / Shift Swap / Open Shift: Process leave and swap requests, revalidate eligibility, manage replacement offers, and prevent race-condition double booking.
FR09 – GPS Attendance & Timesheet: Validate check-in/out against store geofence and configured rules, then calculate reviewable/approvable actual Timesheet records.
FR10 – Dashboard, Reports, Notifications & AI Support: Provide operational statistics, exports, notifications, service/job status, and an optional AI Assistant that explains verified backend data.
Non-Functional Requirements (NFR):
NFR01 – Performance: Core API target P95 < 500 ms in the agreed test environment. Scheduling jobs for the target weekly dataset (approximately 20–30 employees) use a configurable maximum solver time of 15 seconds.
NFR02 – Security & Privacy: Passwords use one-way hashing; protected APIs require secure tokens and authorization; role-based authorization is enforced; GPS is collected only for attendance and internal service access is protected.
NFR03 – Data Integrity & Concurrency: Transactions/Optimistic Locking prevent double booking; published schedules and approved timesheets are versioned or audited; money values use fixed-precision storage.
NFR04 – Fault Isolation & Graceful Degradation: Stopping AI Service must not break core APIs; stopping Scheduling Service must not prevent manual scheduling, attendance, leave, timesheet, dashboard, or reports.
NFR05 – Maintainability & Observability: Use Layered Architecture in Laravel and separate optional services; internal APIs are versioned; each service provides /health; logs/jobs use request_id for tracing; failed jobs can be retried safely.

Public health, safety, and welfare
Bad schedules can cause excessive hours, insufficient rest, or unsafe workload.
Treat overlap, approved leave, working-hour limits, and required rest as hard constraints/configured policy rules; do not trade them away merely to cover a shift.
Social and Global
The design may later serve multiple stores or business types while the Capstone implementation remains focused on Vietnamese F&B.
Keep the MVP single-store; use configurable rules, versioned internal APIs, and modular service interfaces so multi-store support can be added later without changing the core workflow.
Cultural
Part-time staffing and shift flexibility are common in the target F&B context, but policies differ by business and regulation.
Keep working-hour/rest/attendance rules configurable and document assumptions instead of hard-coding unsupported legal values.

The system includes Web, Mobile, Laravel Core, queue, optimizer, optional AI, and monitoring, which increases technical complexity.
Prioritize the core operational workflow first; isolate optional services; maintain automated tests, logs, health checks, and documented interfaces.
8.1. AI/GPT Ethics and Responsible Use
GPT or another external LLM may be used only as an optional assistant. The Laravel/ Microsoft SQL Server core remains the source of truth. AI output is treated as untrusted advisory content and must never directly determine an official schedule, reward, penalty, salary change, promotion, dismissal, or other employment outcome. The project applies the following governance rules:
Table 4. AI/GPT Ethics and Responsible Use
Ethics Area
Responsible-Use Rule / Acceptance
Human oversight
GPT/AI is advisory only. Manager must review and confirm official schedules and all employment-related actions. AI cannot directly publish schedules or change reward, penalty, pay, promotion, or dismissal records.
Privacy & data minimization
Send only the minimum authorized data needed for the request. Do not send passwords, access tokens, secrets, or unnecessary raw GPS coordinates to an external LLM.
Fairness & non-discrimination
Employee suggestions use explicit operational factors such as availability, skill, leave, working-hour limits, and rest rules. Protected or irrelevant personal characteristics are not used for ranking.
Accuracy & hallucination control
Authoritative numbers are calculated by Laravel/SQL Server. GPT may explain verified values but must not invent records, silently overwrite data, or be treated as the source of truth.

AI-generated content is identified as a suggestion. AiRequest/AiSuggestion status, user/role context, timestamps, and request_id are logged so outputs can be reviewed.
Academic integrity
GPT-assisted drafting, coding, or analysis is reviewed by the team. External facts are verified and cited, and AI assistance is disclosed according to course/project requirements.
Target Users/Stakeholders
Primary Users
Store Manager in a small or medium F&B store: responsible for employee management, staffing, schedule approval, attendance review, labor visibility, and day-to-day operational decisions.
Shift-based Staff, especially part-time employees: users who need a clear channel to register changing availability, receive schedules, check in/out, request leave or swaps, and review their actual working time.
Admin: operates the Smart Scheduling platform, manages system accounts and configuration, monitors service health, and maintains platform-level settings without routinely managing the store’s daily staff schedule.
Benefits for primary users:
Reduce repeated manual reconciliation of availability, staffing requirements, skills, leave, and working-hour rules by generating constraint-aware draft schedules and clearly identifying unfilled shifts.
Improve operational continuity: if the optimization or AI service is unavailable, the core Laravel/ Microsoft SQL Server workflow and manual scheduling remain usable instead of making the whole system unavailable.
Improve transparency through published schedule versions, GPS attendance, approved timesheets, auditable overrides, and verified-data dashboards that support labor-cost and staffing decisions.
Technology Stack
Web Frontend:
React.js: Management Web application for Admin and Manager, including employee management, calendar scheduling, dashboards, reports, and service status.
Responsive CSS / component library: Ensures management screens work across common desktop and tablet/mobile browser sizes.
Axios / Fetch API: All Web requests communicate with the Laravel REST API; the browser does not call Python or AI services directly.
Mobile Application and Core Backend:
React Native (JavaScript/JSX): Staff mobile application for availability, schedules, GPS check-in/check-out, leave, shift swap, replacement offers, timesheets, and push notifications.
Laravel (PHP): Core REST API and business layer for authentication, RBAC policies, employee/shift/leave/attendance/timesheet logic, reports, job orchestration, and data validation.
Redis: Queue and cache for asynchronous Scheduling/AI jobs, notifications, retries, and short-lived operational data.
Authentication & Authorization: Short-lived access token/JWT strategy with revocable refresh/session handling, RBAC, and role-based policy checks.
Database and Optional Services:
Microsoft SQL Server: Authoritative relational database for core business data, including:
Users/Roles, single-store configuration, Employees, contracts/pay rates, positions, skills, and availability.
Operating hours, holidays, shifts, requirements, schedule versions, assignments, and scheduling rules.
Leave, swap/open-shift offers, GPS attendance, timesheets, notifications/device tokens, audit logs, and service/job records.
SchedulingRuns, AiRequests/AiSuggestions, health/monitoring metadata, and report data required by the application.
Microsoft SQL Server transactions, foreign keys, unique constraints, and version fields protect integrity. Optional services do not receive unrestricted write access to the production database.
Optimization, AI, and Integration Services:
Python + FastAPI + OR-Tools CP-SAT: Independent Scheduling Service. It receives validated scheduling snapshots/jobs, applies hard constraints and weighted soft objectives, and returns draft results plus shortage/conflict metadata.
AI Service: Optional service that calls predefined Laravel endpoints to summarize or explain verified permission-filtered data. It has no direct unrestricted SQL access and is never required for core API functions.
System Architecture and Deployment:
Architecture Pattern: Layered Architecture for the Laravel Core plus independently deployable Scheduling and AI services. Dependency direction is Web/Mobile → Laravel → SQL Server/Redis; Laravel invokes optional services only when required.
Asynchronous communication: Long-running Scheduling/AI work is represented by job records (QUEUED/RUNNING/DONE/FAILED/TIMEOUT/CANCELLED), queued through Redis, and observed by polling or notification.
Fault protection: Internal calls use timeouts, retry policy, circuit breaker behavior, idempotency/duplicate-run protection, and resource limits per service/container.
Deployment: Docker Compose runs Laravel, SQL Server, Redis, Scheduling Service, and AI Service as separate processes/containers so high solver/LLM load cannot directly consume the Core API’s entire resource budget.
Development, Testing, and Operations Tools:
Git/GitHub: Version control, branch workflow, code review, and team collaboration.
Postman + automated tests: API, authorization, role-authorization, scheduling-rule, concurrency, and graceful-degradation tests.
Docker / Docker Compose: Reproducible local and demo environment with separate resource boundaries for Core, Scheduling, and AI services.
Figma: UI/UX design for Web and Mobile flows.
Draw.io / architecture diagrams: System Context, logical/deployment architecture, sequence, state, and data-flow diagrams.
Methodology & Development Plan
Our project management method uses the SCRUM framework, which is known for its flexibility and iterative development process. This approach allows us to divide the project into short and manageable stages called sprints. Each sprint focuses on specific features and allows the team to adjust the work based on stakeholder needs.
Scrum Process
Figure 1. General SCRUM Process
Scrum is an Agile framework used for software development based on an iterative and incremental approach, aimed at managing software projects as well as product or application development processes. Scrum focuses on project management mechanisms, where an appropriate amount of time is dedicated to planning. This methodology operates on an empirical process control mechanism, using feedback loops as the core management technique, rather than applying a traditional command-and-control management model. Scrum's approach to project planning and management is to empower the execution level with decision-making authority—where the characteristics and practical factors of the work are best understood.
Technically, Smart Scheduling uses React.js for the management Web interface, a React Native Mobile App for Staff, Laravel/ Microsoft SQL Server as the always-available core, Redis for asynchronous jobs, Python/FastAPI + OR-Tools for scheduling optimization, and an optional AI Service for verified-data decision support. This separation allows the team to test the business workflow independently from the intelligent services and to demonstrate graceful degradation during failures.
The Scrum process also includes explicit risk and quality checkpoints: constraint rules and test datasets are reviewed each sprint; role-authorization and concurrency scenarios are automated; service health and failed jobs are monitored; requirement changes are recorded in the backlog; and high-risk integrations are tested early with mocks before end-to-end integration.

System Architecture Overview
System Architecture
The Smart Scheduling architecture is centered on an always-available Laravel Core rather than on the optimizer or AI. React Web and the Staff Mobile App communicate only with the Laravel REST API. Laravel owns authentication, authorization, business rules, data validation, attendance, timesheet, reporting, and official schedule state. Microsoft SQL Server is the authoritative data store, while Redis supports queues/cache and asynchronous processing.
Core Data Layer: users and roles, single-store configuration, employees, contracts/pay rates, skills, availability, operating hours, shifts, schedule versions/assignments, leave/swap/open-shift records, attendance, timesheets, notifications, job status, and audit logs.
Presentation Layer: React Web for Admin/Manager and Mobile App for Staff. Clients never directly access the database, Scheduling Service, or AI Service.
Core Business Layer: Laravel implements RBAC policies, manual scheduling, validation, publishing, leave/swap workflows, geofence attendance, timesheet approval, dashboards/reports, notifications, job orchestration, and fallback replacement filtering.
The Scheduling Service and AI Service are independent enhancement services. Laravel creates a SchedulingRun or AiRequest, queues work asynchronously, and stores the service result as Draft/Suggestion data. Official business data changes only after validation and, where required, Manager confirmation.
Fault isolation is a mandatory architecture rule. If AI Service is stopped, all core functions and dashboard/report calculations continue. If Scheduling Service is stopped, Manager can still create, edit, validate, and publish schedules manually. Internal calls have timeout and circuit-breaker protection, and each service exposes a health check.

Graceful Degradation
AI unavailable → AI Assistant shows temporarily unavailable; core workflow and reports remain operational.
Scheduling unavailable → Auto Schedule is unavailable/queued, but manual scheduling and all core operations remain operational.
Internal services use /health, timeout, retry/circuit-breaker protection, versioned internal APIs, and request_id tracing.
Scheduling results and AI outputs are stored as Draft/Suggestion records and become official only through controlled core workflows.

Conclusion
Smart Scheduling is proposed as a practical workforce-operations system for Vietnam’s F&B sector, not merely as an automatic timetable generator. The iPOS.vn & Nestlé Professional 2025 report shows a market of about 329,500 F&B outlets, strong cost pressure, and increasing demand for professionalized operations. The project responds by integrating structured employee/availability data, configurable shift rules, constraint-based scheduling, GPS attendance, timesheets, and verified operational reporting into one workflow.
The design deliberately protects the core workflow from intelligent-service failures. Laravel/ Microsoft SQL Server remain the system of record and support manual operations at all times; OR-Tools optimization and AI decision support are independent enhancements. This architecture enables the project to demonstrate both technical depth—constraint optimization, asynchronous jobs, concurrency control, fault isolation, and graceful degradation—and practical value for F&B workforce management.

Brief description of product ideas
Smart Scheduling is a Web and Mobile workforce-management ecosystem for small and medium F&B businesses. It collects employee availability and skill/contract data, supports manual scheduling, and uses an independent OR-Tools Scheduling Service to generate optimized draft schedules subject to operational constraints. Published schedules feed a Staff Mobile App for notifications and GPS attendance; attendance feeds timesheets and reports. Leave, shift swap, and open shift workflows help Managers handle changes. Laravel/ Microsoft SQL Server form the always-available core, while Scheduling and AI services are optional enhancements with asynchronous jobs, health checks, timeout/circuit-breaker protection, and graceful degradation. AI only explains verified, permission-filtered data and does not make final employment or scheduling decisions.
